## Technical Skill

Students will identify known values, select the correct relationship, solve for a missing value, and explain why the answer makes sense.

## Student Task

You will analyze a simple DC circuit scenario.

You will:

1. Identify the known values.
2. Identify the missing value.
3. Choose the correct relationship.
4. Show your calculation steps.
5. Include the correct unit.
6. Explain why the answer is reasonable.
7. Identify one verification step that would matter in the field.

## Materials and Tools

- assignment sheet
- calculator
- pencil or digital workspace
- formula reference if allowed
- sample circuit values
- teacher-provided example problem

## Safety Lens

Energy awareness and verification before action.

Even when working on paper or simulation, identify what would need to be verified before this became a real circuit.

Name one condition that should not be assumed in the field.

## Leadership Lens

Peer explanation.

Before submitting, explain your reasoning to a partner.

Your explanation should include:

- what you knew
- what you solved for
- what relationship you used
- why the answer makes sense

## Executive-Function Scaffold

Evidence check and error prediction.

Before solving:

- list the known values
- list the missing value
- predict one common mistake
- explain how to avoid that mistake

## Documentation Habit

Record the following:

- known values
- missing value
- selected relationship
- calculation steps
- final answer with unit
- reasonableness check
- one common error to avoid

## Reflection Prompt

What part of the problem helped you know which relationship to use?

What mistake would you watch for next time?

## Teacher Notes

Common errors to watch for:

- mixing up voltage, current, and resistance
- using the wrong unit
- writing only the answer without showing reasoning
- skipping the known and missing setup
- treating a calculated answer as automatically safe in the real world

Teacher reminders:

- identify what is known first
- write the unit with the value
- explain the answer, not just calculate it
- connect theory back to field verification
