# Conduit Bending Vertical Slice

## Purpose

This is the first course-specific proof slice for the Electrical Trades Program OS.

The goal is to show how one real course can use the assignment compiler to produce a complete, teacher-reviewed assignment package.

## Course

Conduit Bending

## Course Role

Conduit Bending builds physical layout skill, measurement discipline, planning, sequencing, accuracy, tool use, and pride in visible workmanship.

## Why This Course First

Conduit Bending is a strong first proof slice because it combines:

- hands-on trade skill
- measurement
- sequencing
- tool use
- visible quality
- safety awareness
- planning
- correction and revision
- professional pride

## Starter Unit

Basic 90-Degree Bend Layout and Execution

## Technical Skill

Students plan, mark, and complete a basic 90-degree conduit bend using correct measurement, tool positioning, and quality checks.

## Student Task

Students will prepare a piece of conduit for a basic 90-degree bend, mark the conduit based on the assigned measurement, perform the bend, and check the result for accuracy and workmanship.

## Materials and Tools

- conduit
- hand bender
- tape measure
- marker or pencil
- work surface
- assignment sheet
- measurement criteria
- teacher-approved safety expectations

## Safety Lens

Pre-task hazard prediction and tool readiness check.

Student prompt:

Before beginning, identify one thing that could go wrong during bending and one action that helps prevent it. Check that the bender and work area are ready before starting.

## Leadership Lens

Quality check partner.

Student prompt:

Before final review, ask a partner to check one measurement, bend angle, or mark location. Be prepared to explain what you checked and what changed.

## Executive-Function Scaffold

Start plan and sequence check.

Student prompt:

Before starting, list the first three steps. After completing the bend, compare your plan to what actually happened.

## Documentation Habit

Students record:

- assigned measurement
- mark location
- final result
- one correction or adjustment made
- one quality check performed

## Rubric Criteria

| Criteria | Evidence of Success |
|---|---|
| Measurement accuracy | Mark and bend location match assignment expectations. |
| Bend quality | Bend is clean, controlled, and close to required angle. |
| Safety awareness | Student identifies a realistic risk and prevention step. |
| Planning | Student lists steps before beginning and follows a clear sequence. |
| Peer check | Student participates in a partner quality check. |
| Documentation | Student records measurement, result, and reflection clearly. |

## Reflection Prompt

What part of the bend required the most attention? What would you check sooner next time?

## Teacher Notes

Possible common errors:

- rushing the measurement
- marking from the wrong reference point
- incorrect bender orientation
- overbending or underbending
- skipping the final quality check

Possible teacher reminders:

- measure before bending
- confirm tool orientation
- use controlled pressure
- check the result before calling the task complete
