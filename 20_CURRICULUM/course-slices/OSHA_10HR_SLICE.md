# OSHA-10hr Vertical Slice

## Purpose

This is a course-specific proof slice for OSHA-10hr.

The goal is to show how the Electrical Trades Program OS supports safety language, hazard recognition, worker rights, employer responsibilities, reporting expectations, and safe work conduct.

## Course

OSHA-10hr

## Course Role

OSHA-10hr establishes baseline safety language, hazard recognition, worker rights, employer responsibilities, reporting expectations, and safe work conduct.

## Why This Course Matters

OSHA-10hr is not only a certification or compliance block.

It provides safety grammar for the whole Electrical Trades program.

This course helps the OS support:

- hazard recognition
- reporting language
- procedure-following
- worker rights
- employer responsibilities
- non-retaliation awareness
- stop-and-ask habits
- safe work conduct

## Starter Unit

Hazard Recognition and Reporting Basics

## Technical Skill

Students identify a workplace hazard, explain why it matters, and describe the appropriate reporting or response step.

## Student Task

Students will review a simple workplace scenario, identify the hazard, explain the risk, and describe the appropriate action or reporting step.

## Materials and Tools

- teacher-provided scenario
- OSHA-10hr notes or approved reference
- assignment sheet
- pencil or digital workspace

## Safety Lens

Pre-task hazard prediction and reporting awareness.

Student prompt:

Identify the hazard, the possible consequence, and the appropriate action or reporting step.

## Leadership Lens

Care for shared safety.

Student prompt:

Explain how your response protects more than only yourself. Identify who else could be affected by the hazard.

## Executive-Function Scaffold

Stop point.

Student prompt:

Identify the point where a worker should pause before continuing. Explain what needs to be checked or reported.

## Documentation Habit

Students record:

- hazard identified
- possible consequence
- immediate safe action
- reporting or response step
- who may be affected
- one prevention idea

## Rubric Criteria

| Criteria | Evidence of Success |
|---|---|
| Hazard Recognition | Student identifies the hazard accurately. |
| Risk Explanation | Student explains why the hazard matters. |
| Response Step | Student identifies an appropriate action or reporting step. |
| Shared Safety | Student recognizes impact on others. |
| Documentation | Student records the hazard, consequence, and response clearly. |

## Reflection Prompt

Why is reporting or speaking up part of safety? What can happen if a hazard is ignored?

## Teacher Notes

Possible common errors:

- naming the injury but not the hazard
- skipping the response step
- treating safety as only personal preference
- ignoring how hazards affect others
- not connecting safety to procedure

Teacher reminders:

- keep tone clear and serious
- avoid joking with compliance content
- focus on procedure, prevention, and shared responsibility
